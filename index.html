The SimpliGarden method

Three rules.
No perfect garden required.

We are not here to make you feel behind. We are here to help you notice what is already happening, do one useful thing, and enjoy the bit where things grow.

  1. 01

    Start with one square metre.

    Small is not a compromise. A single pot, a windowsill, or one bed is enough to learn from.

  2. 02

    Work with the season.

    Do the right job at the right time. The garden is already keeping a calendar; we just translate it.

  3. 03

    Choose the low-fuss option.

    No-dig beds, drought-tolerant plants, and a little mulch. Good gardening should give you energy back.

The seasonal edit

What to do
this month.

A short list, not a guilt trip. Pick one thing. That is a good garden day.

March – May
  • ✓
    Sow hardy herbs

    Parsley, chives, and coriander can go straight into pots once the worst of the frost has passed.

  • ✓
    Start tomatoes indoors

    Give them warmth and light now for a head start. Pot on when the first true leaves appear.

  • ✓
    Mulch your beds

    A 5cm layer of compost or leaf mould locks in moisture and keeps weeds down.

  • ✓
    Check your watering kit

    Clean cans, check hoses, and set out a rain barrel before the first dry spell.

The one thing

Start tomatoes indoors

Six weeks before your last frost is the sweet spot. A sunny windowsill and a little patience will do.

June – August
  • ✓
    Water deeply, less often

    Early morning is best. Soak the roots rather than giving everything a light sprinkle.

  • ✓
    Deadhead flowers

    Pinch off spent blooms to keep annuals flowering for longer. Leave a few seedheads for wildlife.

  • ✓
    Harvest little and often

    Courgettes, beans, and salad are happiest picked young. Regular picking keeps plants productive.

  • ✓
    Shade young seedlings

    A scrap of horticultural fleece or a light cloth is enough to stop them scorching.

The one thing

Water deeply, less often

One good soak a week encourages roots to go deeper than a daily drizzle ever will.

September – November
  • ✓
    Plant garlic

    Choose a sunny spot and plant cloves pointy-end up. They will overwinter and be ready next summer.

  • ✓
    Sow a cover crop

    Field beans or grazing rye protect bare soil and keep nutrients from washing away.

  • ✓
    Clear leaves into leaf mould

    Fill a bin bag, add a few holes, and leave it behind the shed for a year. Free soil improver.

  • ✓
    Order spring bulbs

    Plant daffodils and alliums before the ground gets too cold. The good varieties sell out early.

The one thing

Plant garlic now

One of the easiest crops you can grow. Plant cloves in autumn, then mostly forget about them until summer.

December – February
  • ✓
    Plan next year's garden

    Sketch what worked, what did not, and the three things you actually want to grow.

  • ✓
    Prune fruit trees

    On a dry, frost-free day, remove dead or crossing branches while trees are dormant.

  • ✓
    Ease off houseplant feeding

    Most houseplants grow slowly in low winter light. Water less and skip the feed until spring.

  • ✓
    Clean and sharpen tools

    Oil the joints, scrub off soil, and sharpen blades. Spring-you will be grateful.

The one thing

Plan, don't plant

Use the quiet months to choose next year's projects. A good plan is the cheapest garden tool you own.
